Sales & Returns Analysis · Project Overview

Customer-provided dataset

Nibal Sharrouf executed this data integration project in March 2026 on behalf of a client requiring unified sales data from multiple subsidiaries. The goal was to ingest, clean, and enrich company-wide sales records to enable cross-company analytics and visualization.

The workflow involved consolidating annual sales tables (2015–2017), performing rigorous data quality checks, cleaning missing keys, and finally building an enriched master table that joins customer, product, territory, and returns information. All transformations were implemented in BigQuery SQL and documented step by step.

Business Objective

Create a single source of truth named final_sales that includes complete customer names, product details, regional territories, category hierarchy, and return quantities. This table serves as the foundation for executive dashboards and cross-company performance analysis.

  • Consolidate fragmented sales tables (2015, 2016, 2017) → unified sales table
  • Identify and remove incomplete records (null customer/product keys)
  • Enrich with dimensional data: customers, products, categories, territories, returns
  • Ensure data completeness and integrity for reporting

Data Pipeline · Step by Step

The following images document the SQL transformation journey — from raw yearly tables to the final enriched dataset, exactly as executed in BigQuery.

1. Union of yearly sales tables
This script creates the base Sales_Data.sales table by stacking data from three yearly tables (Sales_2015, Sales_2016, Sales_2017) and adds a year column to preserve origin. This is the foundational consolidation step, ensuring all historical transactions are available in one place.
2. Detecting duplicate records
Data quality check that groups sales by ProductKey and OrderDate to flag any duplicate transactions. This query ensures no accidental duplication from the union step; duplicates would indicate ingestion errors that need to be resolved before cleaning.
3. Threshold-based filtering (exploratory)
Exploratory query filtering sales where OrderQuantity ≤ θ. This was used to understand order size distributions before final cleaning. The parameterized approach helped define business rules for excluding unrealistic order volumes in later stages.
4. Creating cleaned sales table
Builds Sales_Data.sales_clean by removing rows where CustomerKey or ProductKey is NULL, and ensures OrderQuantity > 0. This eliminates incomplete and invalid records, improving data reliability for downstream joins.
5. Validation: Missing keys summary
After cleaning, a summary table confirms that out of 56,046 total rows, there are zero missing customers and zero missing products. This guarantees referential integrity before the final enrichment step.
Integrity check passed – Total rows: 56,046 | Missing customers: 0 | Missing products: 0
6. Building final enriched sales table
Final transformation that creates Sales_Data.final_sales. It joins cleaned sales with Customers, Products, Subcategories, Categories, Territories, and Returns. The query also constructs CustomerName (concatenated first/last) and handles missing returns with IFNULL(ReturnQuantity,0). This table is ready for visualization and business intelligence.

Visualizations & Business Report

1. Executive Overview

This page provides a high-level summary of the business performance across all categories, regions, and time.

The dashboard shows that the company generated a total Revenue of 24.96M and achieved a Profit of 10.48M, resulting in a strong Profit Margin of 42%. The total number of orders reached 86K, while the Return Rate stands at 11%, indicating a moderate level of product returns.

From the category distribution, it is clear that Bikes dominate the revenue, contributing the vast majority of total sales, while Accessories and Clothing contribute only a very small portion.

Regionally, Australia is the top-performing market, followed by other regions with gradually lower contributions. This suggests that revenue is concentrated in a few key regions.

Looking at the revenue trend over time, the business experienced strong growth from 2015 to 2016, followed by a slight stabilization in 2017. This indicates that the company reached a peak and maintained performance afterward.

The top products are primarily from the Bike category, confirming that high-value items are the main revenue drivers.

Overall, this page shows that the business is highly dependent on a single category (Bikes) and a few strong regions.

2. Category Performance

This page focuses on understanding how each product category behaves in terms of revenue, orders, and pricing.

Accessories

Accessories have very high order volume, making them the most frequently purchased products. However, their contribution to revenue is very low.
This is explained by their low average price of 19.10.

This indicates a high-volume, low-revenue model, where customers buy frequently but spend little per order.

Bikes

Bikes generate very high revenue, despite having relatively low order volume.
This is due to their very high average price (~2000$ per unit).

This makes Bikes the main revenue driver, representing a low-volume, high-value model.

Clothing

Clothing performs very poorly compared to other categories, with very low revenue and very low order volume.

This indicates that Clothing is underperforming in both demand and pricing, making it the weakest category in the business.

Subcategory Analysis

Within Bikes, subcategories such as Road Bikes and Mountain Bikes dominate revenue, while other subcategories contribute minimally.

Orders vs Revenue Insight

A key insight from this page is the imbalance between order volume and revenue:

  • Accessories → High orders, low revenue
  • Bikes → Low orders, high revenue
  • Clothing → Low orders, low revenue

This highlights different business models across categories.

3. Business Recommendations

Based on the analysis:

Accessories Strategy

Since Accessories already have strong demand, the company should:

  • Slightly increase prices
  • Introduce bundling with Bikes

This can increase revenue without losing customers.

Bikes Strategy

Bikes should focus on increasing order volume:

  • Launch marketing campaigns
  • Offer discounts or financing options
  • Promote bundles (Bike + Accessories)

Goal: Increase quantity while maintaining high revenue per unit.

Clothing Strategy

Clothing needs improvement in both areas:

  • Increase marketing visibility
  • Adjust pricing strategy

This category requires a complete performance boost, both in demand and value.

Final Conclusion

The business operates under three different models:

  • Accessories → High volume, low value
  • Bikes → Low volume, high value
  • Clothing → Low volume, low value

The strongest opportunity lies in:

  • Increasing Accessory pricing
  • Increasing Bike sales volume
  • Improving Clothing performance overall

By applying these strategies, the company can significantly improve total revenue and balance its category performance.
