Overview

This report examines how casual riders and annual members use Cyclistic bikes differently, based on 2019 trip data. The objective is to identify actionable strategies to convert casual riders into annual members.

Case brief: Cyclistic is a Chicago‑based bike‑share company. Customers who purchase single‑ride or full‑day passes are casual riders; those with annual memberships are members. Finance analysts have found members are more profitable, and the marketing director aims to convert casual riders.

Data: 3,818,004 trips (Jan–Dec 2019), cleaned to 2,709,828 rows. Source: Motivate International Inc. (public data).

1.

Ask

Business task: Understand how casual riders and annual members use Cyclistic bikes differently. The insight will guide marketing designed to convert casuals into members.

Key questions: When do they ride? How long are their trips? How do patterns differ by day, hour, month, and rider type?

2.

Prepare

Data is publicly available, stored securely, and follows ROCCC principles (reliable, original, comprehensive, current, cited). It includes anonymous trip records: start/end time, station names, ride duration, and user type (casual or member). The cleaned dataset contains 2.7 million trips.

4.

Analyze & Share

This section presents the key findings from the 2019 data. Each graph is accompanied by a description of what it shows.

Weekly Riding Patterns

Casual riders: Weekday rides range from 60,000 to 90,000. Weekend days are significantly higher: Saturday 155,000, Sunday 115,000 — indicating leisure use.

Members: Weekday rides are 320,000–350,000, with lower weekend usage (Saturday 200,000, Sunday 180,000) — consistent with commuting.

Hourly Riding Patterns

Members have two distinct peaks: early morning (7–8 AM) and late afternoon (5–6 PM) — typical commuter hours. Casual riders show activity building through the day, with the highest point at 6 PM and sustained use through the afternoon and evening.

Monthly Riding Trends

September has the highest ridership (0.48M rides). There is a steady decline through October (0.38M), November (0.18M), and December (0.16M) — reflecting seasonal weather impact.

Trip Duration Distribution

For casual riders, the highest number of rides occur around 300 seconds (5 minutes) with 5,000 rides. A secondary peak appears around 150 seconds (20,000 rides). As duration increases beyond 300 seconds, ride frequency gradually decreases.

Rider Type Composition

Casual riders account for 0.62 million rides (22.92% of total). Members account for 2.09 million rides (77.08%). Members make up the majority of trips, but the casual segment (23%) represents a meaningful conversion opportunity.

Gender Distribution by Rider Type

Among casual riders, 38% of rides are associated with a specific gender category. Among members, 1% of rides are associated with a specific gender category. Male members represent the highest group at 45%.

5.

Act

Recommendations based on 2019 insights:

  • Launch weekend marketing campaigns targeting casual riders, highlighting membership savings (since casuals ride most on weekends).
  • Introduce a “weekend‑only” or off‑peak annual pass for casual users who primarily ride on weekends.
  • Add an in‑app feature that compares per‑trip costs versus a membership — show casual riders potential savings.
  • Send targeted emails to casual riders who take frequent long weekend trips during warmer months.
